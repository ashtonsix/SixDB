## Current conversation: distinct ideas and their exploration

Source U is Ashton's request and corrections in this task on 2026-09-26. The
examples are suggestive; their purpose is to expose the general problem. This
table explicitly retains details that could otherwise disappear during summary.

| ID | Recorded idea | Exploration and boundary | Concrete treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U01 | Optimize amplification, reliable delivery, cost, latency and broader service outcomes jointly | A policy can reduce bytes merely by losing work, or improve completed p99 by starving slow recipients. Preserve offered obligations and resource costs, then compare the Pareto set. | [Design space](DESIGN-SPACE.md); all studies retain offered/completed/unfinished work. |
| U02 | All nine one/few/many source/destination combinations | Cardinality alone is insufficient: sources can be equivalent copies or distinct contributions; receivers can be mandatory subscribers or alternative executors. Deduplicate equivalent copies by identity; aggregate distinct contributions only with the workload's law and exact coverage. | Main message matrix; [algorithms](ALGORITHMS.md), [sender comparison](ADAPTATION.md). |
| U03 | Read scale-out across shards and within replicas of a shard | Partition logical coverage at one usable cut; select ready replicas without multiplying required scans. Tiny fragments improve balance but add dispatch, reduction and cancellation costs. | [Reads](READS-AND-EXTENSIONS.md), read cases and checks. |
| U04 | Small packets are expensive; batch at fan-in relays | Packet batching preserves all records; semantic combining may replace them. Waiting for a full batch at light load hurts latency, while a dense frontier map may exceed its tiny updates. | Incast sweep, timer/byte controls and exact frontier guards. |
| U05 | Programmatically inject faults and increase payload/rate to latency degradation or overflow | Finite CPU, NIC, memory, persistence, queue, retention and reassembly resources expose different cliffs. Marginal percentile sums and an infinite queue do not describe a joint tail. | [Model](MODEL.md), all three physical stress regimes and fault checks. |
| U06 | Relays can enhance messages, with plans only one example; exact filter matches also count | Place legal transforms jointly with routes. The useful form can shrink, expand or replace information. Resident receivers may prefer a tiny predicate request over a larger selection; nonresident receivers still need result rows. | [Enhancement](ENHANCEMENT.md): exact selectors, residency, sparse/dense/runs, sidecars and edge class. |
| U07 | Inputs such as events and admission arrive separately; delay forwarding to analyze both | Shared analysis begins only after its real inputs arrive. Blocking joins add latency and hold bytes; optional analysis spends resources even when nobody uses it. Joining before an expensive edge pays only if downstream saved work exceeds join/encoding/transfer cost. | Main blocking/optional fixture, prefix and relay studies; generalized guarded workflows. |
| U08 | Unequal edge costs can change where enhancement belongs | Charge representation bytes on the actual directed edge, including cold input acquisition and reverse replies. Moving compute to a cheap relay can save money while increasing total bytes or latency. | Enhancement cold-relay and [edge economics](EDGE.md). |
| U09 | Multiple starting points and readiness-adjusted synthetic root for Edmonds | A virtual source encodes released alternatives, not free extra payload copies. Independent evidence/contributions cannot be collapsed into interchangeable roots. A scalar tree objective does not model joins or correlated readiness. | Exact shortest/Edmonds oracles, release-time examples and [algorithms](ALGORITHMS.md). |
| U10 | 170µs p99 / 250µs p99.9 internal effect; fast follower is the producer follower | Treat the topology as an opportunity for overlap. Compare independent payload/journal work, effect, client return and background coverage separately. Exact post-persistent effect semantics remain open; the early proposal candidate is not the current brief. | Strict/candidate write and prefix studies, assumed service sensitivity; no hardware SLA claim. |
| U11 | Small shards, large shards, shared witnesses, hosts participating in several shards | Logical edges contend on physical hosts. Sharing witnesses reduces deployment resources but couples independent load; uniform healthy total throughput can hide one blocked producer. | Shared/separate witnesses, many producers, large consumer fanout and per-producer prefix traces. |
| U12 | Co-located roles can communicate by roughly 100ns memory handoff | Preserve identity/receipt semantics while replacing a network edge with memory/CPU work. The propagation assumption alone excludes cache coherence, cross-NUMA transfer, poll duty, queue contention and waking a sleeping core. | Same-node memory path and CPU sharing; no measured 100ns end-to-end guarantee. |
| U13 | In-arborescences can aggregate on frontiers | Per-source contiguous progress combines by max; safe all-reader reclamation combines by min; query contributions require exact nonduplicated coverage. Max received sequence skips holes and is invalid. | Delivery/frontier histories and packet/semantic comparison. |
| U14 | Rings: send request to one server, receive response from another | Carry request identity, reply route/capability and completion obligation independently of the ingress connection. Large results reverse the favorable direction of a small edge-origin request; late/cancelled replies still occupy resources. | Read rings, mixed extension returns and edge return-size sweep. |
| U15 | Significant backend applications can run as SixDB extensions | A tiny invocation can discover shards, scan, iterate, expand outputs or perform effects. Placement must include code/state warmth, mandatory checking, nonpreemptible grain and caller backpressure, not just request size. | Workload survey, [mixed traffic](MIXED.md), checked-effect/interaction counterexamples. |
| U16 | Perturb edge weights, form a family of trees, pick among them for load balancing | Noise can create chains and shared bottlenecks. Constrain candidates, then choose at a useful byte/time grain; stable hashes do not guarantee short-window balance. | [Adaptation](ADAPTATION.md): exact family setup work, fixed/hash/interleaved/coarse choices. |
| U17 | Maintaining/distributing many trees may be costly as flows change | Route descriptions need installation, versioning, rollout and retirement. Warm setup is not recurring maintenance. Per-message route metadata trades distribution for data-plane bytes; a stale version must still own its outstanding obligations. | Family install cost; delivery old-route/new-member history; analytical transition discussion in network survey. |
| U18 | Hierarchical/subnet delegation can reduce prescribed detail | Delegate a regional coverage obligation and resource budget, not just a gateway name. Scopes can overlap rather than form one tree; topology summary must not hide congestion or correlated failure. | Shallow/regional route comparison and generalized overlapping scopes. |
| U19 | Relocate witnesses and direct clients toward suitable servers continually | Moving traffic, data, computation and admission authority have different switching costs. Compare delayed observations, cold movement, old/new overlap and benefit horizon; instantaneous least-loaded selection can herd traffic. | [Design space](DESIGN-SPACE.md), recorded layout/recovery alternatives; continuous controller remains unimplemented. |
| U20 | Edmonds may be inappropriate because chains and fanout affect the critical path | Minimum edge sum is a useful baseline, not the application objective. Shallow trees still overload if forwarding concentrates; shortest paths can spend excessive source packets/egress. | Exact cost/depth counterexamples plus loaded direct/cheap/shallow family comparisons. |
| U21 | For many-to-one/few, select a sender using pre-agreed randomness | Rank equivalent eligible copies consistently, or use ownership/receiver credits/readiness estimates. Entropy cannot reconcile different candidate views or prove liveness. Backup notices consume time; they cannot recall an in-flight packet. | Sender ranking checks and network-backed healthy/crash/receipt-loss comparisons. |
| U22 | Target failure; retries are not a general solution | Retry can recover lost transfer to a live holder. It cannot recreate a unique destroyed input, prove a lost external-effect outcome, or change authority. Congestion retries can make failure worse. | Delivery histories, source incarnation checks, ACK-loss and retry overload cases. |
| U23 | Newly added VMs must receive required messages despite old routes | Define subscription cut and snapshot/tail coverage before claiming the new member is current. New physical read replica does not add a new logical partition. Bounded retention may force refusal or another durable holder. | Five network-backed join cases and bounded retention tests. |
| U24 | Generalize all suggestions and explore broadly rather than fixating on the follower example | Choose derivation, required execution count, sender, representation, release, retention, physical assignment and observation policy independently, then test their interactions. One helpful example must not become an accidental deployment rule. | [Design space](DESIGN-SPACE.md), four source surveys and the catalog's explicit evidence distinctions. |
| U25 | Recover old edge-origin broadcast and cost asymmetry; survey all recorded ideas | Changing where the same information originates can eliminate the first expensive transfer. That is distinct from choosing a cheaper tree after cloud egress is already sunk. Record adjacent NAT/blob/private-link ideas individually. | Network survey N entries and [edge study](EDGE.md); catalog introduced in response to the missed passage. |
| U26 | Health-guided early versus eligibility-before-proposal timing, relayed by Orbital LEAD from Ashton during the survey | Use locally observed missing/slow receipts and received warnings to change future proposal timing. Preserve existing allocated positions. A cooldown, small early window or healthy other producer cannot erase an old prefix hole; false alarms pay strict-path latency. | [Proposal timing](PROPOSAL-TIMING.md), bounded observer and network-backed comparisons; conditional-entry consensus remains unadopted. |

| ID | Recorded source | Generalization and exploration | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | [Network-first placement and provisioning; many clouds as one](../../../../consurgent/pitch/SCRIPT.md), lines 11–21 | Optimize placement and capacity together. Cheap CPU can be uneconomic when moving retained state, cold warming, egress, double-running and authority transfer exceed savings. Over horizon H, require saved resource cost over H to exceed those one-time and recurring costs; H itself is uncertain. Placement constraints can make the cheapest route illegal. | Economic boundary, not a provisioning algorithm. Edge study varies origin/relay, not capacity purchase. |
| P02 | Same passage: resilience, sovereignty and infrastructure loss | Placement is constrained by declared independent failure domains and permitted data movement, not merely region labels. Separating metadata from payload may permit one path where another is disallowed, but metadata can itself contain protected information and remote authority can still block local progress. | Domain/evidence distinctions in delivery and model; no legal classification or sovereignty guarantee. |
| P03 | [Staging, derivation and certificate circulation](../../../../consurgent/pitch/SCRIPT.md), lines 49–93 | Prepare/transfer before final release where possible; replicate canonical inputs and derive state; gather attestations then broadcast a compact certificate. Compare saved state transfer with repeated compute, verification, certificate production and recovery. A digest cannot establish arbitrary program correctness, and old BFT majority rules do not replace the current native-checking obligation. | CFT and xmem reports are historical bounded measurements. Current study tests work overlap/replication and guards, not the pitch's BFT protocol or numerical claims. |
| P04 | [Summaries tell descendants what can affect the result](../../../../consurgent/pitch/SCRIPT.md), lines 101–105 | Carry pruning evidence down the work graph so children avoid irrelevant work. Conservative filters reduce candidates but leave exact verification; exact empty coverage is a result. Maintaining summaries, false positives, cut mismatch and layout changes can erase savings or correctness. | Exact selection experiment plus workload survey's row-filter, regexp and aggregate-maintenance evidence. |
| P05 | [Logical/physical identity and adaptive representation](../../../../consurgent/pitch/SCRIPT.md), lines 105–107 | Permit replicas to store different usable encodings while transferring logical results/references with versioned interpretation. A physical bitmap or pointer does not automatically survive remapping. Warmth and conversion costs can outweigh a compressed transfer. | Selection binding/remapping checks; layout/workload survey. No universal inherited Calico container geometry. |

These ideas connect economics to execution and recovery. They do not require a
hypervisor, a universal simulator, BFT, a particular cloud, or a compute market
implementation in the dissemination spike.
