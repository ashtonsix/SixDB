# Network source survey

This is a source audit and exploration, dated 26 September 2026, for the broad
dissemination spike. It is not an adopted Orbital design. **Recorded** below
paraphrases a source; **analysis** is this survey's interpretation, benefit
condition and counterexample; **coverage** names evidence or a concrete missing
comparison. A historical assertion is not implementation evidence. Historical
prices, product claims and latency estimates have not been revalidated here.

The omission that prompted this audit is substantive: [old BRIEF](../../../orbital/stale-drafts/BRIEF.md)
lines 7 and 76–82 records origin-sensitive cross-provider broadcast, direct
database-to-destination traffic, NAT processing, cheap bulk plus direct hash
verification, private-link fixed costs and small-object peer fetching. These
are separate ideas, not merely different labels on an AZ graph.

## Gaps that change the next experiment

The strongest missing **transport** comparisons are N17–N19 (packet sizing,
loss recovery and FEC), N58–N63 (physical pacing, semantic credits and resources
that remain charged during cancellation), and N52–N57/N70 (observed adaptive
control with stale information and switching cost). They cannot be discharged
by another static tree or a current simulator run with different edge latency.

The strongest missing **representation/application** comparisons are
N20–N25/N39–N40/N48–N50: baseline/dictionary availability, verification,
transcoding and code/plan reuse need to travel with their byte savings.
The neighboring workload and delivery catalogs own those interfaces.

For each of these, a useful first result can be negative or conditional. For
example, parity may lose under congestion; bigger MTU may help packet CPU while
failing after a path change; a cheap relay may need an expensive first copy;
receiver-ready selection may double traffic after lost ACKs; and distributed
compute may save network while worsening CPU and recovery. These are concrete
comparison axes, not claims that every mechanism belongs in the brief.
