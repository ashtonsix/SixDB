# Turning technical advantage into durable customer value

SixDB's commercial question is how a generalist database could turn exceptional
technical performance into a lasting improvement in what customers can deliver
and operate, and support a business they continue to choose. The working
hypothesis is that one engine can reduce both the computational cost of useful
work and the integration and coordination needed to make that work possible.
Whether customers can capture those benefits, and what they will pay for them,
remain open.

Ashton clarified on 27 September 2026: **database first; the broader compute
vision remains an option to investigate**. An enterprise platform or market
for compute must earn its scope through useful applications. The older
[Consurgent pitch](../../../consurgent/pitch/SCRIPT.md) is historical context,
not current positioning, financing terms or delivery commitments.

## Technical strength leaves a commercial question

The initial discussion granted a database exceptional benchmark results,
strong simultaneous workloads and convincing operational evidence. Integration
maturity, live adoption and customer discovery remained weak or absent. The
founder also expected competitors to close much of the performance gap within
roughly three years. Even on those favourable technical assumptions, the
commercial conversion was uncertain: a valuable invention was not yet evidence
of a repeatable business.

That remains a useful thought experiment. It directs attention to adoption,
trust, economics and the use of a possibly temporary lead, rather than treating
another benchmark win as an answer to every commercial question. Those were
valuation assumptions, not claimed SixDB results or a company forecast.
The [current project](../../README.md) is a clean rebuild;
[Calico](calico.md) is prior work, and customer evidence has not been established
in this discussion.

Four questions connect performance, adoption and the business:

| Question | What would make the advantage commercially meaningful? |
| --- | --- |
| What becomes better for a customer? | A useful outcome, lower equivalent-service cost or a capability previously impractical |
| Can the customer adopt and depend on it? | A manageable change to systems, skills, responsibilities and operating risk |
| Why would the customer continue choosing it? | Benefits, trust and useful relationships that persist as alternatives improve |
| Can SixDB sustain the offer? | A paid offering and support burden compatible with the resources available |

## Find an outcome a buyer can capture

Candidate benefits include predictable application latency during analytics,
fresher decisions, less paid capacity, fewer integration failures and less
operator work. A large speedup can matter directly to a constrained workload;
elsewhere its value may be headroom, simpler operation or a new product feature.
The comparison must include SixDB's price, migration, temporary duplication,
support and the customer's actual alternative, with equivalent completion,
freshness and recovery promises.

Infrastructure simplification is therefore a hypothesis about a whole use.
Higher event throughput alone does not replace Kafka's surrounding functions;
connectors alone do not establish that an existing pipeline can be retired.
Some integration machinery can disappear while its schema, access, freshness
and ownership agreements remain necessary. Savings count when paid usage,
capacity or work can actually be removed. The
[network survey](dissemination/SOURCE-SURVEY-NETWORK.md) supplies useful examples
of why fewer bytes need not reduce a bill; its tariffs are illustrative.

Choose discovery around a concrete workload, owner and reason to change.
Finance and healthcare remain possible contexts, not established target
markets: placement controls do not alone establish compliance or willingness
to buy. The more useful question is which constraint a particular buyer cannot
meet acceptably today, and what adopting SixDB would require of them.

## Adoption changes an operating arrangement

The engine may be general while the first adoption is narrow. Distinct
analytics and application explanations could help existing teams evaluate it.
A product team owning both an operational application and live reporting could
also capture HTAP value within one business capability. An internal platform
team offers another route, with different consumers and operational demands.
Compare benefits and burdens across the buyer, operators and consuming teams.
These are alternatives to investigate, not a chosen customer profile.

The [sociological note](sociology.md) connects three aspects of that change:
Team Topologies asks about delivery and cognitive load; Polanyi about
independent discovery and shared scrutiny; Ostrom about decision rights and
shared obligations. Resistance may protect legitimate knowledge and
accountability. Separate landing pages cannot compensate for making a team
dependent on unfamiliar operators or transferring decisions it needs to own.

The positioning hypothesis is to make useful data work easier while preserving
clear responsibility for changes and decisions. A first team should gain
standalone value; later sharing should preserve that value and expose its
costs. Common tooling, selective sharing and separate deployments may coexist.
Performance headroom could make stronger resource boundaries affordable.
Consolidation need not require an enterprise reorganisation or one authority
over every use of data.

## What can endure as alternatives improve

If a performance lead narrows, customers need continuing reasons to choose
SixDB. Candidates include dependable operation, easier upgrades and diagnosis,
useful integrations, responsive support and accumulated understanding of real
workloads. An ecosystem could add independently developed tools and expertise.
These advantages require operating experience; they cannot be inferred from
an architecture or a promise of eventual platform breadth.

Defensibility also has an internal side. Understanding why the architecture
works, whether additional engineers can maintain it, and how fixes and
compatibility evolve matters to delivering the offer. A reproducible binary
reduces uncertainty about observed behaviour without answering all of those
questions. Secrecy, patents and an assumed inability of incumbents to retrofit
are insufficient grounds for treating a lead as permanent. Its duration and
the pace of commercial learning remain empirical questions.

Trust can include the freedom to inspect, extend, move data or change provider.
Those freedoms may help adoption while reducing switching barriers. The
business should investigate whether customers stay because useful service and
relationships improve, rather than assuming dependency itself establishes
customer value. The sociological analysis develops this distinction between
shared infrastructure and control over its participants.

CedarDB/Umbra prompted a useful comparative question: how does technical merit
become routine use? They should remain subjects for investigation, not assumed
proof of commercial failure. Distinguish academic or evaluation availability
from an adoptable product, and public attention from customer evidence.
Likewise, ClickHouse and Databricks are comparisons to examine, not evidence
that SixDB will follow the same trajectory.

## Connect evaluation, distribution and the paid offer

Benchmarks can earn attention and make a technical claim testable. Evaluation
must also let a prospective adopter explore the relevant semantics, integration
and operating behaviour. Architecture discussion and source inspection answer
different questions from running a supplied binary. Claims should say what was
measured, under which conditions, and what remains untested; reproducible
unofficial results should not be presented as certified benchmarks.

Open source is a serious distribution and trust hypothesis. It may support
evaluation, adaptation and contributions while exposing ideas to competitors.
The useful timing question is what an interested user can adopt or buy when
attention arrives, and whether SixDB can deliver and support it. Opening code
without a usable offer can waste interest; delaying access can also delay
learning and adoption. No licence or launch timing is settled here. Evaluation
access, source availability, open source, portability and project governance
are distinct choices.

Candidate paid value includes reliable operation, support and integrations that
remove consequential work. Packaging and pricing should follow an identified
buyer and cost of service. A pilot that works through continual founder
intervention is useful evidence, but its support burden must be understood
before treating it as a repeatable offer. Commercial features earn their place
through customer needs, not an abstract enterprise checklist.

## Capital, learning and company value

The earlier question about a small initial angel cheque raised a useful
distinction between funding customer learning and funding a scaled company.
If the next useful work is discovery and limited pilots, a large team may add
commitments before it adds learning. Conversely, prolonged unpaid work and an
unrealistically small operating budget can prevent even those experiments
from being completed reliably. Founder runway, support time and unavoidable
costs belong in the account of what an experiment requires.

The valuation question depends on the technology and execution evidence, the
remaining commercial uncertainty and the actual financing proposal. Benchmark
rank alone does not supply an equity price; the size of a cheque is not a
valuation of the whole opportunity. Earlier hypothetical valuation estimates
and stale pitch terms are not company decisions. This note sets no financing
terms, incorporation plan or fundraising target.

## Commercial work and evidence

Commercial LEAD connects capability, customer outcomes, adoption and the paid
offer. The remit includes discovery, positioning, migration and pilot proposals,
economics, distribution and competitive questions. Ashton sets company
direction and commitments; external customer or investor contact requires his
instruction. Technical owners retain architecture, correctness and measurement
judgment. Consult them directly and return useful customer constraints to their
work, following the [collaboration guidance](../collaboration.md).

Orbital supplies judgment about placement, recovery and execution dependencies;
Engine, Loom and Shore work supplies semantics, scheduling and integration
judgment as it develops. Scaffolding helps keep evidence discoverable. Details
stay with their owners: [Orbital's brief](../../orbital/BRIEF.md) contains
targets, [CFT measurements](../spikes/cft-commit-latency/RESULTS.md) report
component results, and the [simulator](../simulator/README.md) explores authored
models. These are not interchangeable with production or customer evidence.

Follow real changes and attempted adoption: who wanted an outcome, what blocked
it, what they tried instead, and what they could pay for or stop doing. Let
findings revise the thesis. Technical research need not begin with a prescribed
sales application, and commercial judgment should not turn promising results
into guarantees. The purpose is to find a useful business that the technology
can sustain, while remaining open to where the evidence leads.
